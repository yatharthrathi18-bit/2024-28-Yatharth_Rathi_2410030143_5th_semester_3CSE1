About the Simulation

The Solutions Architecture Job Simulation was a practical,
client-oriented learning experience focused on translating business
requirements into a suitable technology solution.

The main practical task recorded on my Certificate of Completion was:

Assess the Client's Needs and Design a Solution

The experience followed a client-first approach rather than beginning
with a particular technology. The goal was to understand the problem,
identify requirements and constraints, evaluate solution options, and
communicate a high-level recommendation.

Objectives

The key objectives of the simulation were to:

Understand the role of a Solutions Architect in a client-facing
technology environment.

Assess business goals, client needs, requirements, and constraints.

Translate business requirements into technical requirements.

Consider scalability, security, reliability, performance, and cost.

Develop a high-level solution architecture.

Evaluate architectural trade-offs.

Communicate technical recommendations clearly to stakeholders.

Strengthen analytical thinking, problem-solving, and professional
communication.

Solution Architecture Approach

The approach used throughout the simulation can be summarized as:

Client Needs
     ↓
Requirements & Constraints
     ↓
Solution Options
     ↓
Trade-off Evaluation
     ↓
High-Level Architecture
     ↓
Recommendation
     ↓
Stakeholder Communication

1. Understand Client Needs

The first step is to understand:

Business objectives

User needs

Existing challenges

Constraints

Expected outcomes

2. Analyse Requirements

Business needs are translated into functional and non-functional
requirements.

Important considerations include:

Security

Scalability

Reliability

Performance

Cost

Implementation complexity

3. Design the Solution

A high-level architecture is developed based on the identified
requirements. Technology choices should have a clear purpose and should
directly support the client's needs.

4. Evaluate Trade-offs

Architecture decisions often involve balancing competing priorities. A
solution should not optimize one factor while unnecessarily compromising
others.

5. Communicate the Recommendation

The final solution should be explained in a way that both technical and
non-technical stakeholders can understand, including its benefits,
limitations, risks, and major design decisions.
